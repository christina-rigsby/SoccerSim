SoccerSim Self-Play Lab

Self-Play Lab

How SoccerSim's play generator learns. Teams first play each other with only the twelve hand-written plays (Phase A). A critic learns what each play is worth in each situation, and a generator learns to write plays in the play language (Phase B). Then the generator competes in a league against copies of itself, exploiters and scripted styles (Phase C). At match time it is called when no hand-written play fits the situation.

Phase A · library self-play → critic + response model → Phase B · BC + mutation filtering → Phase C · PPO in a league → QD archive → promoted plays (human review)

AWhere the play library runs out

For every decision our team made with the ball, Module 2 checked every library play against its triggers, hard constraints and role requirements. When no play except the always-available recycle play fits, or the best one scores under the generator threshold, that is a library gap, and it is when the generator is consulted.

CQuality-diversity archive and promotion

Every generated play the league executes is filed by where it starts, its tempo, number of passes and objective. Each niche keeps its best play once it has enough evaluations. Elites that beat the library median in their niche against every scripted style are exported for human review.

RunsHow each run builds on the last

Every run mutates and imitates the plays earlier runs promoted, and seeds its archive with them, so a new play has to beat them in their niche. A run continues training the previous run's generator only if the opponent pool is unchanged and the previous run passed its regression gate: its final generator was not worse than its starting one against any scripted style.
